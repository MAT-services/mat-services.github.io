# MAT Services — Organisation & Governance Framework **Version:** -0.3 — Working Document **Status:** Preliminary — Not officially adopted ## 1. General Overview MAT Services is an organization structured into branches and sub-branches, administered through a hierarchical organizational tree. The organization has a central administration responsible for its overall coordination, supervision and management of shared matters. Branches may carry out different activities and pursue different objectives. They do not automatically constitute legally independent entities. The organizational structure, branches and their respective characteristics are documented separately to facilitate their development and evolution. ## 2. Organizational Structure ### 2.1. Central Organization MAT Services has a central administration responsible for, among other things: - Coordinating the organization as a whole. - Supervising the different branches. - Managing shared resources. - Coordinating the various administrations. - Managing administrative posts across the organization. - Making decisions concerning the organization as a whole. ### 2.2. Branches Branches are organizational subdivisions of MAT Services. They may have: - Their own administrations. - Sub-branches. - Specific posts and responsibilities. - Distinct objectives and areas of activity. - Specific operating rules. - Budgets or resources allocated to them. Detailed information about each branch is maintained in its respective documentation. ### 2.3. Branch Hierarchy The complete structure of branches and sub-branches is documented in a separate document: **MAT Services — Branch Tree** This document serves as the reference for consulting the organization's current structure. It provides an overview of the hierarchical relationships between branches without requiring this document to be updated every time the organizational structure changes. ## 3. Entities and Posts The administrative organization is structured as a hierarchy. An entity may represent, among other things: - A person holding a function. - An administrative post. - An administration. - A collective body, such as a parliament. - A branch or sub-branch. - Any other organizational structure recognized by MAT Services. An entity may contain multiple other entities. Definitions and terminology distinctions are maintained in the **MAT Services — Definitions & Glossary** document. ### 3.1. Hierarchical Relationships By default, a superior entity has hierarchical authority over its subordinate entities. This authority includes, in particular, the ability to oversee and manage subordinate entities. The powers actually exercised depend, among other things, on: - The nature of the entity. - Its associated responsibilities. - The powers assigned to the relevant posts. - Any explicitly defined restrictions or exceptions. Certain posts may therefore have limited access to the control functions of their own entity. A post cannot exercise authority over entities or posts positioned above it in the hierarchy. Exceptions to the usual hierarchical relationship may be explicitly defined. ### 3.2. Individuals and Positions An individual may hold several positions simultaneously. Responsibilities and powers are associated with the relevant positions and entities, rather than automatically belonging to the individual independently of their assigned functions. A position may remain vacant and exist independently of its holder. ## 4. Administration and Delegation Administrations are responsible for managing their respective areas. Each administrative level has authority over its subordinate entities, within the limits of the powers granted to it. The central administration retains overall authority over the entire organization. ### 4.1. Assignment and Definition of Powers Powers are individually assigned to each post. Their definition may rely on predefined documents, which posts may reference in whole or through specific sections of those documents. These references are used to define the responsibilities, powers and restrictions applicable to each post. The powers of a post remain subject to the limits imposed by its hierarchical position. If multiple documentary provisions applicable to the same post contradict one another, the affected provisions are blocked until the contradiction has been clarified. No automatic priority is applied to resolve such conflicts. ### 4.2. Information Escalation and Approval Important information, situations and requests must be communicated to the next hierarchical level. Whenever a decision requires higher-level approval, the request is progressively escalated through the hierarchy until it reaches the competent authority. The receiving authority may then approve or reject the request. ### 4.3. Post Management The central administration is responsible for the overall management of all posts within MAT Services. Superior entities may also manage posts belonging to their subordinate entities, subject to the powers granted to them. Such management may include: - Creating posts. - Appointing holders. - Reassigning holders. - Removing responsibilities. - Reorganizing posts. The creation of a post involving specific responsibilities or external commitment powers requires authorization from the competent superior responsible person. ### 4.4. External Commitments and Responsibilities A branch administration may undertake external commitments when one or more responsible individuals have been expressly designated for this purpose and have accepted this responsibility. If no authorized responsible person has been designated, any request involving a legal commitment must be escalated to a higher level until it reaches a post with the necessary authority. Internal organizational powers do not, by themselves, constitute legal authorization to bind an external individual or organization. The legal requirements for representation and signing remain subject to the legal status applicable to MAT Services. ## 5. Branch Activities and Objectives Branches may pursue different objectives. These may include: - Non-profit objectives. - Commercial objectives. - Informational, community or promotional objectives. - Technical, educational or other objectives. Potential commercial activities do not automatically change the objectives of other branches. The creation of commercially oriented branches is being considered as part of the future development of MAT Services. The objectives and specific characteristics of each branch are described in their respective specifications, where available. ## 6. Financial Management The financial management of MAT Services is defined and administered by the central administration. ### 6.1. Budget Allocation The central administration may allocate budgets to certain branches according to their needs. These budgets may be subject to restrictions defining their permitted uses. Branches must comply with the conditions associated with the resources allocated to them. ### 6.2. Financial Reserves A branch may request the creation of a financial reserve intended to receive funds allocated to it. A reserve has a target amount. The relevant funds are allocated to it until the specified target amount is reached. Reserved funds may only be used in accordance with their intended purpose and the applicable authorizations. ### 6.3. Use of Resources Using MAT Services funds or allocated budgets for unauthorized personal purposes is strictly prohibited. ## 7. Legal Development MAT Services is currently an unregistered organization. Formal legal registration is being considered as part of its professional development. The future legal status should, as far as possible, allow MAT Services to retain: - Its central organization. - Its administrative hierarchy. - Its branches and sub-branches. - The diversity of its activities. - A financial management structure suited to its different branches. This document does not constitute legal statutes and does not grant legal personality to the entities described herein. ## 8. Collective Bodies Collective bodies may constitute administrative entities in their own right. Their specific operating rules may be defined in dedicated documents when their creation or operation makes this necessary. ## 9. Complementary Documents This document is part of a broader documentation framework that includes, among others: - **Definitions & Glossary**: Common definitions and terminology. - **Branch Tree**: Representation of the administrative hierarchy. - **Branch Specifications**: Descriptions and specific rules for each branch, where necessary. - **Administrative Procedures & Powers & Responsibilities**: Administrative procedures and detailed definitions of powers and responsibilities. - **Financial Management**: Detailed financial management rules. - **Legal Representation**: Legal representation and commitment procedures, once the applicable legal status has been determined. These documents may evolve independently while remaining consistent with the general rules established in this framework. --- **Note:** This document describes the known operating principles and organizational direction of MAT Services. It must not be interpreted as formal adoption of all the provisions it contains.
